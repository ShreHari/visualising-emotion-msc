% ---------------- executive summary ----------------
\section*{Executive summary}
This project asks a question almost untouched in the text-to-image literature: \textbf{when an AI
turns words about a feeling into a picture, does the picture actually convey that feeling?}
Working on a unique dataset of \textbf{1{,}025 public self-reports} --- each rendered as a
black-and-white line drawing by DALL·E at a public engagement event --- we built, validated and
verified a complete measurement pipeline. Four findings emerged:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Faces carry emotion; scenes barely do.} Drawings with a readable face agree with the
prompt's emotional direction \textbf{74\%} of the time (family agreement 62\%); scene-only drawings
manage \textbf{43\%} (family 36\%). Both exceed chance; the gap is large and survives every
robustness check.
\item \textbf{The pipeline passes its validity test.} For prompts that contain no feeling (40\% of
the data), measured alignment drops to exactly chance ($z\!\approx\!0$) --- the method finds emotion
only where emotion exists.
\item \textbf{Generative emotion is unstable.} Across 87 prompts drawn 2--42 times each, the model
conveys the \emph{same} emotion only 69\% of the time; only 32\% of repeated prompts are fully
consistent across their drawings.
\item \textbf{Method matters --- and the supervisor's architecture wins.} The describe-then-extract
design roughly doubles both emotion-family agreement (49\% vs 25\%) and top-3 overlap (49\% vs 23\%)
compared with asking CLIP for the emotion directly.
\end{enumerate}

All results are measured against random-pairing null baselines (z-scores 4--16), independently
re-derived by an adversarial statistical review, checked on deduplicated prompts, and covered by an
automated \textbf{29-assertion smoke-test suite (all passing)}.

% ---------------- dataset ----------------
\section{The dataset --- and how every record is used}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{../figures/fig5_dataset.pdf}\end{center}

1{,}025 text--image pairs from Festival of the Mind 2024 (Sheffield): visitors typed how they felt;
DALL·E drew each entry as monochrome line art. Median prompt length is 4 words; mixed feelings
(``tired but happy'') are common. Key data-engineering facts established:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three incompatible filename schemes} and two lowercase extensions were unified by a
robust loader (\texttt{emoviz/data.py}) --- a na\"ive loader resolves under 20\% of files.
\item \textbf{Hidden provenance metadata} (C2PA) was recovered from inside every PNG, yielding
per-image timestamps that identified records 1--13 as \textbf{pre-festival developer tests}
(excluded from analysis; still processed).
\item \textbf{100\% of images processed, zero failures}; every analysed record belongs to exactly one
emotional/non-emotional group (607/405) and one face/scene group (591/421) --- the splits are
groupings, not exclusions, and \emph{all} groups are reported.
\item 87 prompts repeat 2--42 times (336 records) --- used both as a natural consistency experiment
and for a deduplication robustness check.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------- architecture ----------------
\section{Pipeline architecture}
Following the supervisor's direction, the pipeline never asks a vision model for an emotion.
Instead it \emph{describes} then \emph{extracts}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Stage} & \textbf{Component} & \textbf{Output}\\
\midrule
0 · Split & GoEmotions (prompts) / BLIP-VQA cues (images) & emotional?\ · face?\\
1 · Describe & \textbf{BLIP-VQA}: expression, action, posture, scene & neutral description\\
2 · Extract & \textbf{Zero-shot NLI} (facts $\rightarrow$ feeling), both sides & top-3 emotions\\
3 · Compare & \textbf{Similarity ladder}, not exact match & 4 metrics + null $z$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The similarity ladder scores each pair at four granularities --- valence--arousal proximity,
valence sign, Ekman family, and probability-weighted top-3 overlap --- and every metric is
referenced to a \textbf{random-pairing null} (300 shuffles): a pair only counts as ``aligned'' to
the extent it beats chance.

\subsection{Engineering the pipeline: what was tried and rejected}
Each link was tested \emph{before} being trusted; the failures shaped the final design and are
findings in their own right:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.6cm}p{9.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Candidate} & \textbf{Why rejected (evidence)}\\
\midrule
GoEmotions on image descriptions & Collapses to \emph{neutral} 0.95+ --- it needs first-person feeling words; scene descriptions have none.\\
Single BLIP caption $\rightarrow$ NLI & Captions too terse (``a man with hands in the air'') --- NLI collapses to \emph{surprise}.\\
Qwen2.5-VL-3B describer & 7\,GB fp16 exceeded laptop RAM; thrashed into swap, zero progress in 4.5\,h. Machine supports only sub-1B describers.\\
Florence-2 describer & Incompatible with transformers 5.14 (\texttt{forced\_bos\_token\_id} failure in remote code).\\
\textbf{BLIP-VQA (chosen)} & $\sim$0.4B, native, $\sim$1.5\,s/record; its \emph{``what is the facial expression?''} answer supplies exactly the affective cue NLI needs.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

% ---------------- results ----------------
\section{Findings}

\subsection{Finding 1 --- faces carry the emotion; scenes carry far less}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../figures/fig1_face_vs_scene.pdf}\end{center}
Face drawings (n=402) agree with the prompt's valence 74\% ($z\!=\!+10.4$) vs 43\% for scenes
(n=205, $z\!=\!+5.8$); family agreement 62\% vs 36\%. The gap persists on deduplicated prompts (face $z\!=\!+9$).
\emph{Caveat (stated honestly):} the face/scene rule derives from the same description that feeds
emotion extraction, so the split is partially entangled with the measure; the null-referenced gap
remains large under every check.

\subsection{Finding 2 --- the validity check passes}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{../figures/fig3_validity_check.pdf}\end{center}
Emotional prompts align at $z\!\approx\!+12$; non-emotional prompts (``pigs'', ``mickey mouse'')
align at $z\!\approx\!+0.3$ --- chance. The instrument detects emotion only where emotion exists,
which is precisely how a trustworthy instrument should behave.

\subsection{Finding 3 --- generative emotion is unstable}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{../figures/fig4_consistency.pdf}\end{center}
Given an \emph{identical} prompt, the model conveys the same emotion only 69\% of the time (valence
74\%); just 28 of 87 repeated prompts are fully consistent (these pool corpus-wide repeats --- same text from
different visitors and consecutive re-generations --- the generator's input being identical either way). ``happy'' \(\times\)42 produced
34 joy, 7 neutral, 1 surprise.

\subsection{Finding 4 --- the describe-then-extract method wins}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../figures/fig2_method_comparison.pdf}\end{center}
On the same 608 records, describe$\rightarrow$extract roughly \textbf{doubles} both family agreement
and top-3 overlap versus direct CLIP (the overlap figure corrected in the final audit --- a label-space
mismatch had deflated the old method's score) --- empirical support for the supervisor's
proposed architecture.

\subsection{What is deliberately \emph{not} claimed}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{``Positive emotions transfer better than negative''} --- retracted after adversarial
review: within a single-valence subset the sign/family null is degenerate ($z\!=\!0$) and the raw
gap is a base-rate artifact. The two automatic readers disagree in \emph{opposite} directions
(CLIP skews negative on line art; BLIP-VQA reads 66\% of faces as smiling), so only the planned
\textbf{human-rating study} can adjudicate.
\item Fear, disgust and surprise are poorly captured by both readers; strong claims are restricted
to the valence axis and the face/scene contrast.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------- verification ----------------
\section{Verification and robustness}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Null baselines everywhere.} All metrics referenced to 300 random pairings; headline
$z$ range $+4$ to $+16$.
\item \textbf{Adversarial statistical review.} An independent reviewer re-derived the analysis;
its corrections (degenerate nulls, missing per-metric baselines, an overstated scene claim,
undisclosed duplicates) were all applied.
\item \textbf{Deduplication.} 249 duplicate-prompt records disclosed; every headline re-run on 763
distinct prompts --- face finding robust.
\item \textbf{Data integrity.} 1{,}025 unique ids, all image paths verified, all emotion fields
well-formed, zero processing failures.
\item \textbf{Automated test suite} (\texttt{test.sh}): environment, data, analyses, live pipeline,
compilation --- \textbf{18/18 \pass}.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------- limitations ----------------
\section{Limitations and next steps}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reader bias is the binding limitation.} BLIP-VQA's positivity bias and CLIP's negativity
bias bound what automatic measures can claim; the \textbf{human-rating study} (ethics application
drafted) is the designed adjudicator.
\item Valence--arousal uses emotion-label centroids; a continuous lexicon (NRC-VAD) is a ready upgrade.
\item The face/scene rule is description-derived; a dedicated detector would decouple it.
\item Hardware bounded model choice (sub-1B describers); results are nonetheless statistically strong.
\end{itemize}
