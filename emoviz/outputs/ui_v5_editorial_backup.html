Does the drawing feel what the words felt?

At a science festival, people typed how they felt and an AI drew each one. This instrument runs the research pipeline live — it describes a drawing, reads the feeling from that description, then measures how close the two are.

Method and limits

Describe, don't ask

Asking a vision model “what emotion is this?” is unreliable on line art. This pipeline never does: one model reports only observable facts — expression, action, scene — and a separate language model infers the feeling from them.

Your words are read the same way, so both sides are comparable.

Similarity, not accuracy

Two people rarely agree exactly on an emotion, so the score measures closeness: “happy” and “excited” count as similar rather than as a mismatch.

In the research, every score is referenced against random pairings — alignment counts only when it beats chance.

Known limits

The drawing-reader leans optimistic, and subtle emotions such as fear or disgust are often missed. That is a finding of the research, not a fault of your input.

Nothing you type or upload is stored. Some dataset drawings are withheld to protect the privacy of festival visitors.
