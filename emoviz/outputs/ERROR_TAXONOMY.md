## Reading of the tally

- The describer, not the generator, is where most high-confidence disagreements arise: modes (a)+(b) cover 19 of 38; the drawing shows a feeling other than the prompt's in only 2 of 38 (mode e).
- people_zero is a usable predictor of mode (a): 7 of 9 records tagged (a) have a people-count of 0, against 6 of 29 in every other mode. When BLIP-VQA reports no people yet names an expression, the expression is being invented.
- The joy->neutral cell is a single mechanism: 5 of 6 are mode (c), and in every one the describer answered 'calm' or 'smile' and the reader returned neutral. Low-arousal positive states (grateful, at peace, relaxed) have no family in the 7-way scheme, so 'calm' falls to neutral by construction; and the reader returned neutral for the two 'happy heart' records although the description names a smile twice.
- bare_expression_word fires on 38 of 38: in this population it adds no information, because an image top-1 score >= 0.8 is only reached when the describer gives a one-word expression answer.
- mixed_feeling_connective (but/although/though/yet) catches 0 of the 11 mode-(d) records; its 2 hits fall in other modes. Visitors list feelings without connectives ('tired proud happy', 'hopeful, slightly anxious, resigned'), so a list detector (3+ feeling words separated by commas or spaces) would serve better than a connective detector.
- Across all 1012 analysed records, 107 share a byte-identical five-answer VQA dict with at least one other record (43 groups); ids 93 and 216 in this census are one such pair with visibly different drawings. The describer's answer vocabulary is coarse, and identical descriptions produce identical image readings.

Tie-break rule for records with more than one fault: a failure visible in the drawing itself is primary; (d) is used when the prompt lists several feelings or names none cleanly; (c) when the description is true to the drawing and the label is lost between description and family; (f) when a clear single-feeling prompt lands in the wrong family. Secondary faults are in the per-record `note` field of the JSON.
