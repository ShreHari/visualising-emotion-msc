# Dissertation Review — marker's assessment and required corrections
Prepared 3 September 2026. Every claim below was checked against the code and the analysis outputs, not against the project's own documentation.
---

## 1. Where this sits
HONEST VERDICT — as a Sheffield MSc AI dissertation marker.

WHERE THIS SITS. On the written outputs as they stand, this is a solid upper-second project with a clear route to a distinction that it has not yet taken. The engineering and the framing are distinction-level. The statistics are not, and the gap between how rigorous the documents SAY they are and how rigorous they actually are is the single biggest risk — because the documents draw attention to their own rigour, which invites the examiner to check it, and it does not survive checking.

STRENGTHS (real, not consolation).
1. The question is genuinely novel and genuinely narrow. "To what extent does an automatic image-emotion reader agree with prompt-derived emotion labels for AI-generated monochrome drawings" is a well-formed, answerable MSc question, and the dossier's paragraph explaining why it is deliberately narrower than "does the drawing convey the feeling" is the best paragraph in the project.
2. The dataset is not downloadable, the prompts are real first-person self-report, and the engineering to make it usable — three filename schemes, C2PA timestamp recovery identifying the 13 pre-festival tests — is exactly the kind of unglamorous work that distinguishes a real project from a Kaggle exercise. Recovering developer tests from signing timestamps is a nice piece of forensics and should be foregrounded.
3. "Chance is not zero" is the strongest single idea here. Discovering that the headline metric has a floor of 41% and a random-pairing expectation of 76%, and then rebuilding the reporting around that, is a genuine methodological insight, honestly arrived at, and it will earn credit in the viva.
4. The rejected-approaches table is evidence, not excuse-making. Qwen thrashing 16 GB with zero progress in 4 h 45 min, Florence-2's transformers incompatibility, GoEmotions collapsing on third-person descriptions, a bare caption collapsing to "surprise" — these are findings and they belong in the methods chapter exactly as written.
5. The withdrawal of the positive-versus-negative claim, on the grounds of a degenerate null and two readers biased in opposite directions, is the mark of someone doing research rather than producing a result. Say so out loud in the viva; it is worth more than the finding would have been.
6. Name redaction: reviewed decisions with recorded reasons, the "Jack Frost" over-reach, and the dedup-key fix that stopped two different people's prompts merging into one. That last one is a subtle bug caught properly.
7. Reproducibility infrastructure — fixed seeds, resumable JSONL, a runnable self-test, a live demonstrator — is well above the norm at this level.

WEAKNESSES.
1. There is no inferential statistics in this project. I grepped: no scipy, no bootstrap, no chi-square, no Fisher, no confidence interval, no paired test anywhere in the codebase. Every quantity is a point estimate with a permutation z against its own null. The headline finding is a DIFFERENCE between two groups, and the difference is never tested. "The gap survives every check" names checks that do not exist. The dossier's limitation 6 admits this, which is honest, but admitting a missing analysis does not substitute for running it — and a bootstrap CI on the face-scene difference is roughly forty lines of code and an afternoon.
2. The negative control, which the dossier explicitly nominates as mattering "more than any single result", does not do what it claims. Two of its four metrics are degenerate by construction (every prompt in the subset shares the neutral label, so permutation cannot move family agreement — I reproduced z = +0.0 exactly), and the subset is contaminated: at minimum 15% of it contains an explicit emotion word ("ecstatic", "delighted but tired", "content", "hopeful", "inspired"), nearer 40% by inspection, and two records are the bare token "[name]". The control is not worthless — the VA result at z=+0.3 is meaningful — but it is one metric on a noisy subset, not four metrics on a clean one.
3. The circularity caveat is wrong about its own mechanism. This is the most damaging finding in my review, precisely because it is the caveat. The write-up says scene descriptions "lack expression content"; 96.4% of them contain a readable expression word. The split is driven by the people-count answer, and the actual artifact is that the description template writes "A drawing of none showing...", after which the NLI reader returns neutral for 35% of emotional scenes versus 5% of faces — and a neutral image reading cannot match a signed prompt. Fixing this is straightforward and would materially strengthen the chapter; leaving it will not survive a competent second marker.
4. Two headline findings rest on unvalidated classifier splits. Neither the emotional/non-emotional split nor the face/scene split has a precision or recall figure, despite PIPELINE_DESIGN.md having planned exactly that ("validate on ~50-100 hand-labelled images"). Hand-labelling 100 images and 100 prompts is a day's work and would convert both splits from assumptions into measurements.
5. The consistency finding is the only headline with no null, in a project whose central methodological claim is that every number is quoted against a null. I computed it: the null is 59.4%, so 68.8% is z~+4.4 — still a finding, but a quarter as dramatic as an unbaselined "69%" sounds, and half the groups are pairs with a 50% floor. Worse, the variance is attributed entirely to DALL-E when it also contains BLIP-VQA and NLI noise for which no reliability estimate exists.
6. The architectural claim is softer than presented. "The vision model is never asked for an emotion" is literally true of the question wording and substantively misleading: the expression question returns a bare emotion word for 38% of records, and the "separate" text model reproduces that word's family 69% of the time. The method comparison may therefore be showing that BLIP-VQA reads line art better than CLIP, not that describe-then-extract is a better architecture — and the comparison additionally uses a different prompt reader and a different subset definition from every other headline, which neither document discloses.
7. Cross-document drift. Fourteen quantities are stated differently across the six files, including a factor-of-ten-in-credibility one (66% versus 57% for the same statistic, with the inflated version in the file labelled authoritative), and a claim that the test suite has 28 checks when it has 29 smoke checks that assert no numbers at all.

WHAT WOULD SPECIFICALLY LOSE MARKS.
- Any perception verb surviving into the final text. "Convey" and "carry" in the README, and above all in the three figure titles, contradict the dissertation's own most careful paragraph. A marker who reads the figure titles and then the limitations chapter will conclude the student does not believe their own caveats. This is the cheapest fix in the whole list and the most expensive to leave.
- "Detected automatically and no chance figure is reported for them." It is a hand-passed boolean on two of six code paths, and the z is printed anyway. A false claim about a safeguard is worse than no safeguard, because it converts a limitation into an integrity question.
- "All survive, at z between +9 and +11", contradicted by the same document four pages later. This will read as selective reporting even though the correct number is stated elsewhere.
- "Independently reviewed for statistical validity" with no reviewer, date or scope. This is the first thing an examiner will ask about, and there is no good answer.
- Absent confidence intervals on every reported proportion. At MSc level this is now expected, not optional, and the omission is visible on every table.
- The face/scene headline framed as a claim about faces rather than about a classifier's output. The dossier's own "Stated carefully" paragraph gets this right; the heading above it, the README, the presentation and the figure title all get it wrong. Markers weight headings.
- "Twelve themes" when the bibliography has eight, and "28 tests" when there are 29. Small, but they are the errors a marker can verify in one minute, and they calibrate how much of the rest they will trust.

WHAT TO DO WITH THE REMAINING TIME, IN ORDER.
(1) Bootstrap CIs on every reported proportion and a bootstrap or chi-square test of the face-scene difference — one afternoon, and it removes the single largest weakness. (2) Rewrite the circularity caveat to describe the actual mechanism (people-count clause, "A drawing of none" prefix, neutral-cap on valence sign) — one hour, and it turns a wrong caveat into a genuinely sophisticated one. (3) Compute and report the null for the consistency finding — twenty minutes. (4) Hand-label 100 prompts and 100 images to give both splits a precision/recall figure — one day, converts two assumptions into measurements. (5) Do a global find-and-replace on the perception verbs and the terminology list above — two hours. Items 1-3 alone move this from a good 2:1 to a credible distinction, because they close the gap between claimed and actual rigour, and that gap — not the absence of a human study, which is properly justified — is what is currently holding the work back.

## 2. Claims that must be corrected before they enter the dissertation
These were found to overreach their evidence. Each is given with the correction to use instead.

### 1. "does the drawing convey the feeling in the words?" and "Faces carry the emotion; scenes carry it far more weakly"
**Where:** emoviz/README.md (opening paragraph, and Key results #1); emoviz/outputs/RESULTS_final.md (Headline finding 1 heading)
**Problem:** "Convey" and "carry" are perception verbs — they assert something about a viewer. No viewer exists in this project. The measured quantity is the rate at which a BART-MNLI reading of a BLIP-VQA description lands in the same Ekman family as a GoEmotions reading of the prompt text. The dossier states this correctly ('It does not establish that a human viewer perceives the same feeling'), so the README and RESULTS_final contradict the project's own stated scope rather than merely being loose.
**Use instead:** "Does an automatic image-emotion reader agree with a prompt-emotion reader?" / "Prompt-image label agreement is substantially higher for drawings the describer classifies as containing a person with a readable expression than for those it does not."

### 3. "scene descriptions lack expression content, which mechanically lowers their scores"; "the face/scene split is defined by whether BLIP-VQA reported an
**Where:** emoviz/outputs/RESULTS_final.md (Headline 1, 'Honest caveat — partial circularity'); project_dossier.tex Limitation 2; PRESENTATION_TECHNICAL_STORY.md 'Limits to state yourself'
**Problem:** Factually wrong, and it is the project's flagship honesty statement, which makes it doubly damaging. I checked: 406 of the 421 'scene' records (96.4%) DO contain a readable expression word — 'smile' x161, 'smiling' x92, 'happy' x79, 'angry' x31, 'sad' x20. ### 6. "emotion-family agreement sits exactly on its null" — presented under the heading "Why this matters more than any single result"
**Where:** project_dossier.tex 'A negative control that passes'
**Problem:** It sits exactly on its null because it cannot do otherwise. Every prompt in that subset has top-1 = neutral by the subset's own definition, so family agreement is simply the proportion of image readings that are neutral — a quantity permutation of the image side leaves numerically unchanged. I reproduced it: true 0.286, null 0.286, z = +0.0 exactly; valence sign 0.000 vs 0.000. This is an algebraic identity reported as an empirical validity result. RESULTS_final.md spots this for the valence-sign cell ('a definitional artifact, not a result') but not for family, and the dossier presents both as passing checks. Only VA carries information here, and it gives z=+0.3.
**Use instead:** "On the non-emotional subset only the valence-arousal metric is informative: 76.6% against a null of 76.6% (z=+0.3). Valence-sign and family agreement are degenerate here — every prompt shares the neutral label, so permutation cannot change them and their z is identically zero by construction, not by measurement. The control therefore rests on one metric, not four."

## 4. Terminology — one term per concept
Nine terms are used interchangeably for things that are not the same, and the drift is not cosmetic — in several places it is what produces the over-claim.

1. ALIGNMENT / AGREEMENT / SIMILARITY / FIDELITY / MATCHING / CONVEY / CARRY. All seven currently appear for the central quantity ("alignment" 4x dossier, 6x RESULTS_final, 7x RESULTS_describe_extract; "agreement" 8x/4x/4x; "similarity" 7x/2x/8x; "fidelity" in both results files; "convey" 4x dossier, 2x README). STANDARDISE ON: AGREEMENT, always with both readers named — "prompt-reader/image-reader agreement". Why: it is the only one of the seven literally true of what was computed, two automatic labellers landing on the same label. "Alignment" is borrowed from text-to-image evaluation, where it means correspondence to the prompt's actual content — the thing this project cannot measure. "Fidelity" asserts faithfulness to an original. "Convey" and "carry" assert perception by a viewer, the project's single most important disclaimed limitation, and yet they appear in a figure title. Keep SIMILARITY for one narrow use only — the valence-arousal metric, which really is a graded distance — never as a synonym for the whole ladder; the supervisor's "similarity not matching" framing then correctly describes the metric design, which is what she meant. Retire "fidelity" and "matching" except when saying what the project does NOT do.

2. MOOD. PRESENTATION_TECHNICAL_STORY.md introduces a ninth name — "74% mood agreement", "mood-direction" — for what every other document calls valence-sign agreement. STANDARDISE ON: VALENCE-SIGN AGREEMENT, glossed once as "do both readers land on the same side of pleasant/unpleasant".

3. FAMILY / CATEGORY / CLASS / LABEL. The dossier uses "category" for GoEmotions' 27 and "family" for the collapsed 7; PIPELINE_DESIGN says "Ekman-6 family"; the code says "fam". STANDARDISE ON: CATEGORY for the 28 GoEmotions output labels, FAMILY for the 7 collapsed targets, always written "seven emotion families", never "Ekman-6". Why: the two levels are different objects and the reader must know which one agreement is being measured on.

4. FACE / SCENE. These labels claim more than the rule delivers, and the mislabelling is what produced the wrong circularity explanation. STANDARDISE ON: FACE-CLASSIFIED and SCENE-CLASSIFIED — better still, PERSON-REPORTED and NO-PERSON-REPORTED, since 414 of 421 "scenes" are records where the describer answered "none" to the people question. Why: under the current names, "faces carry emotion" reads as a claim about faces in the world; under the corrected names it reads as a claim about a classifier's output, which is what it is.

5. NULL / CHANCE / BASELINE. "Null", "null baseline", "chance line", "chance level" and "baseline" all appear — and "baseline" additionally means the frozen direct-CLIP method. STANDARDISE ON: RANDOM-PAIRING NULL for the permutation reference (or "chance level" in the presentation, defined once), reserving BASELINE exclusively for the direct-CLIP comparison method. Why: "baseline comparison" currently means two different things two paragraphs apart in RESULTS_final.md.

6. DESCRIBE->EXTRACT / describe-then-extract / "the new method" / "Chen's architecture" / "the supervisor's architecture". STANDARDISE ON: DESCRIBE-THEN-EXTRACT, used every time, with the attribution made once in the methodology chapter. Why: "the supervisor's architecture wins" as a findings heading reads as deference rather than evidence, and the examiner marks the student, not the supervisor.

7. RECORD / IMAGE / DRAWING / PROMPT / ENTRY / SELF-REPORT / "text-image pair". All seven appear as the unit of analysis, and this is exactly what lets "1,025 images" slide into "about a thousand members of the public". STANDARDISE ON: RECORD as the unit (a prompt-drawing pair), DRAWING for the image, PROMPT for the text. Never pair a person-word ("visitors", "members of the public") with a record count.

8. RUN / REPEAT / DUPLICATE / "repeated prompt". data_audit_consistency.py says "runs", RESULTS_final says "repeated prompts", PIPELINE_DESIGN says "duplicate-prompt runs", and a superseded 65-run definition is still in circulation. STANDARDISE ON: REPEAT GROUP (87 of them, 336 records), reserving "duplicate" for the deduplication robustness check only.

9. SIGNIFICANT / z-SCORE. "Significant", "highly significant" and "z" are used as though a permutation z were a p-value, with an explicit "significance threshold (z~2)" drawn in Figure 3 and no correction across roughly forty reported z values. STANDARDISE ON the explicit form: "exceeds its random-pairing null by X standard deviations of the permutation distribution (permutation p < .001, uncorrected)", plus one sentence stating that no multiple-comparison correction has been applied. Why: "significant" without a stated test, direction and correction is the phrase most likely to be circled in red.
