# Annotated Bibliography — Curated Evidence Base

**73 papers** retained after filtering 97 read papers for *relevance* and *evidence strength*. Grouped by function in the dissertation. Excluded papers (surveys, position pieces, off-target work) are in `lit/_excluded/` with reasons in `FILTERING_LOG.md`.

> Filter rule: keep only papers that are both **relevant** (on the dissertation's path) and **evidence-based** (empirical study, or a dataset/benchmark/lexicon resource). Seminal foundations kept by exception.


## 1. Foundations — emotion theory & taxonomy  (5)

- **Posner, Russell & Peterson (2005). *The circumplex model of affect: An integrative approach to affective neuroscience, cognitive development, and psychopathology*.** — Development and Psychopathology (Cambridge University Press) 2005  
  Review arguing the dimensional circumplex model of affect (valence x arousal) better explains affective-neuroscience findings than discrete basic-emotion theory.  
  *Why here:* Supplies the canonical valence-arousal (circumplex) framework the dissertation can use to structure emotions extracted from the short free-text descriptions and to organize/score how faithfully the DALL·E line-art images convey affect (e.g. mapping "tired but happy" into the 2-D space). It also directly engages the supervisor's domain by arguing facial expressions do not cleanly delineate discrete emotions, which frames the image-fidelity evaluation.  
  *Reusable:* Circumplex model of affect (2-D valence x arousal / activation affect space; Russell, 1980) - directly reusable to code and plot the 1,025 emotion descriptions; Valence and arousal (activation) as the two orthogonal dimensions; Multidimensional scaling (MDS) of similarity ratings - the analytic technique used to derive circumplex structure  
- **Machajdik & Hanbury (2010). *Affective Image Classification using Features Inspired by Psychology and Art Theory*.** — ACM Multimedia (MM '10), Firenze, Italy, 2010  
  Classifies still images into eight discrete emotion categories using low-level features derived from psychology and art-theory (color, texture, composition, content).  
  *Why here:* Directly serves the dissertation's strand (b) — evaluating how faithfully images convey emotion — by providing a validated computational pipeline that maps low-level visual features to discrete emotion categories, plus a reusable eight-category emotion scheme (Mikels), a rigorous imbalance-aware evaluation protocol (true-positive-per-class, one-vs-all CV), and a human-agreement baseline. Its composition, texture and content features and the whole methodology transfer to the student's black-and-white DALL-E line-art images (though its color-centric features are largely inapplicable to monochrome art), and its abstract-paintings-vs-human comparison is a strong model for benchmarking image-conveyed emotion against human perception.  
  *Reusable:* IAPS (International Affective Picture System); Artistic photographs dataset (807 images, released at imageemotion.org); Abstract paintings dataset (228 peer-rated images, imageemotion.org); Mikels et al. eight-emotion category word list (Amusement, Awe, Contentment, Excitement, Anger, Disgust, Fear, Sad); Ekman basic-emotion mapping  
- **Cowen & Keltner (2017). *Self-report captures 27 distinct categories of emotion bridged by continuous gradients*.** — PNAS 2017 (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, vol. 114, no. 38, pp. E7900–E7909)  
  A large-scale self-report study showing that reported emotional experience spans 27 distinct categories connected by smooth continuous gradients rather than discrete clusters or a few affective dimensions.  
  *Why here:* Provides the core emotion-psychology taxonomy for the dissertation's text strand: the 27 categories (and the 14 valence/arousal-style affective dimensions) offer a validated, granular label set for classifying the 1,025 free-text emotion descriptions beyond coarse basic-emotion schemes. Its central claim — that emotions live in a high-dimensional space bridged by continuous gradients — directly motivates the project's evaluation of how faithfully DALL·E line-art images convey blended, nuanced feelings like "tired but happy".  
  *Reusable:* Library of 2,185 emotionally evocative short (~5 s) video clips (publicly released); Interactive online map of the 27-dimensional emotion space (https://s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/emogifs/map.html); 27 distinct reported-emotion categories (reusable emotion taxonomy/lexicon: admiration, adoration, aesthetic appreciation, amusement, anxiety, awe, awkwardness, boredom, calmness, confusion, craving, disgust, empathic pain, entrancement, excitement, fear, horror, interest, joy, nostalgia, relief, romance, sadness, satisfaction, sexual desire, surprise, sympathy); 34-category emotion rating set used in data collection; 14 affective-dimension scales (approach, arousal, attention, certainty, commitment, control, dominance, effort, fairness, identity, obstruction, safety, upswing, valence)  
- **Demszky et al. (2020). *GoEmotions: A Dataset of Fine-Grained Emotions*.** — ACL 2020 (Proc. 58th Annual Meeting of the ACL); arXiv 2020  
  A manually annotated dataset of 58k English Reddit comments labeled for 27 fine-grained emotion categories plus Neutral, with a BERT baseline.  
  *Why here:* Directly underpins strand (a) of the dissertation: GoEmotions supplies a psychology-grounded 27-emotion taxonomy, a large labeled corpus, per-emotion lexical markers, and an off-the-shelf BERT emotion classifier that can be reused to extract fine-grained emotions from the 1,025 short free-text descriptions ("tired but happy"). Its taxonomy is explicitly built on Cowen & Keltner's semantic-space work, tying the text-NLP side to supervisor Chaona Chen's facial-expression/emotion-perception framing.  
  *Reusable:* GoEmotions dataset (58,009 Reddit comments, 27 emotions + Neutral; released with all annotator ratings); 27-category fine-grained emotion taxonomy (with sentiment and Ekman 6-category groupings); Top-5 lexical correlates per emotion (Table 3) via log-odds-ratio with informative Dirichlet prior; Macro-averaged Precision/Recall/F1 as the evaluation metric; BERT-base fine-tuned multi-label classifier (reusable baseline)  
- **Raffel et al. (2020). *Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer*.** — Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR) 2020  
  The T5 paper: a systematic study casting every NLP task as text-to-text, unifying pre-training objectives, architectures, and datasets to push transfer-learning limits.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports the dissertation's NLP strand (extracting emotions from short free-text descriptions): T5's text-to-text formulation is a standard, reusable backbone for framing emotion classification or emotion-tag generation as a sequence-to-sequence task, and the released pre-trained models are practical tools for the pipeline. It is, however, an NLP transfer-learning paper rather than a vision-language/captioning model, so it fits the text-analysis side more than the image-faithfulness evaluation side.  
  *Reusable:* C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus); GLUE benchmark; SuperGLUE benchmark; SQuAD (question answering); CNN/Daily Mail (summarization)  

## 2. Text strand — emotion-from-text methods  (10)

- **Tausczik & Pennebaker (2010). *The Psychological Meaning of Words: LIWC and Computerized Text Analysis Methods*.** — Journal of Language and Social Psychology 2010  
  A review of computerized text analysis in psychology describing how the LIWC word-count dictionary was built, validated, and used to detect emotion and other psychological states from text.  
  *Why here:* This is a foundational reference for strand (a) of the dissertation — extracting emotions from the 1,025 short free-text descriptions via NLP. LIWC's positive/negative/anxiety/anger/sadness lexicons offer a validated, transparent, reusable dictionary approach that can be applied directly to the median-4-word emotion phrases and cited as a psychology-grounded baseline against modern embedding methods; it also grounds the choice of emotion categories in work by a psychologist audience (relevant to supervisor Chaona Chen).  
  *Reusable:* LIWC (Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count) 2007 dictionary — 80+ hand-validated word categories; LIWC positive emotion category (~406 words; examples: love, nice, sweet); LIWC negative emotion category (~499 words; examples: hurt, ugly, nasty); LIWC anxiety (~91), anger (~184), sadness (~101) sub-categories; LIWC affective/overall emotion category (~915 words)  
- **Zanwar et al. (2022). *Improving the Generalizability of Text-Based Emotion Detection by Leveraging Transformers with Psycholinguistic Features*.** — arXiv 2022 (preprint, cs.CL)  
  Hybrid models fusing BERT/RoBERTa transformers with a BiLSTM over 435 psycholinguistic features to improve out-of-domain text emotion detection.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports the dissertation's text strand: it is a state-of-the-art recipe for extracting emotions from short free-text via transformers, and its explicit focus on out-of-domain generalizability is pertinent because the 1,025 crowd-authored emotion phrases form a new domain unlike the training corpora. The Ekman/GoEmotions taxonomies, benchmark datasets, and the reusable psycholinguistic feature and affect-lexicon inventory offer concrete methods and baselines for the NLP pipeline.  
  *Reusable:* GoEmotions (Demszky et al. 2020, 58k Reddit comments, 27+neutral categories); ISEAR (Scherer & Wallbott 1994, 7,665 sentences, 7 emotions); Unified Emotion Dataset (Bostan & Klinger 2018); AffectiveText (Strapparava & Mihalcea 2007); CrowdFlower  
- **Grootendorst (2022). *BERTopic: Neural topic modeling with a class-based TF-IDF procedure*.** — arXiv 2022 (preprint, cs.CL)  
  A topic-modeling method that embeds documents with a transformer, clusters them, and extracts topics via a class-based (c-TF-IDF) term-weighting scheme.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports dissertation strand (a): BERTopic offers a transformer-embedding + clustering pipeline to surface latent emotion themes across the 1,025 short free-text descriptions, and its c-TF-IDF gives interpretable per-cluster keywords for labelling. Caveat worth noting in the writeup: the descriptions are very short (median 4 words) and BERTopic/HDBSCAN can struggle with sparse short texts and assign many to an outlier cluster, so parameter tuning or a short-text embedding model would be needed.  
  *Reusable:* 20 NewsGroups dataset (16,309 articles, 20 categories); BBC News dataset (2,225 documents, 2004-2005); Trump tweets archive (44,253 tweets, 2009-2021); UN general debates transcripts (2006-2015); OCTIS (Optimizing and Comparing Topic models Is Simple) evaluation toolkit  
- **Chung et al. (2022). *Scaling Instruction-Finetuned Language Models*.** — arXiv 2022 (arXiv:2210.11416; later published in JMLR 25, 2024)  
  Instruction-finetunes LLMs (Flan-PaLM, Flan-T5) on 1.8K tasks including chain-of-thought, dramatically improving zero/few-shot generalization to unseen tasks.  
  *Why here:* Directly useful to the dissertation's text (NLP) strand: the publicly released Flan-T5 checkpoint introduced here is a lightweight, instruction-following backbone that can do zero/few-shot emotion extraction and classification on the 1,025 short free-text descriptions without task-specific training. Flan-T5 is also the text encoder in several vision-language/captioning models, so it links the project's text-analysis and image-evaluation strands.  
  *Reusable:* MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding, 57 tasks); BBH (BIG-Bench Hard, 27 tasks); TyDiQA (multilingual QA, 8 languages); MGSM (multilingual grade-school math, 10 languages); RealToxicityPrompts (toxicity/Responsible-AI eval)  
- **Yang et al. (2023). *Context Unlocks Emotions: Text-based Emotion Classification Dataset Auditing with Large Language Models*.** — arXiv 2023 (arXiv:2311.03551, cs.CL; work also presented at ACII 2023)  
  Uses GPT-3.5/GPT-4 to synthesize missing context for short texts and re-audit an emotion-classification dataset, improving alignment between texts and their annotated emotion labels.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports strand (a), extracting emotions from short free-text descriptions, by showing that very short inputs (the dissertation's median-4-word phrases like "tired but happy") are frequently under-specified and multi-labelable, and that LLM prompting can both flag context-poor items and enrich them to sharpen the emotion signal before analysis. Its faithful/unambiguous context definition and GoEmotions-based ambiguity examples give the student a principled way to reason about, and mitigate, label ambiguity when mapping terse emotion phrases to categories.  
  *Reusable:* GoEmotions (27 emotions + neutral, Reddit); ISEAR; SemEval 2019 Task 3 (EmoContext); Tweet Eval emotion-recognition subset; Daily Dialog  
- **Liu et al. (2023). *Emotion classification for short texts: an improved multi-label method*.** — Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 2023 (vol. 10, art. 306)  
  An NLP method that adapts the Multi-label K-Nearest Neighbors (MLkNN) classifier with iterative corrections to improve multi-label emotion classification of short Twitter texts.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves dissertation strand (a) — extracting emotions from text via NLP — by supplying a concrete multi-label emotion-classification pipeline for very short texts, which matches the median-4-word emotion descriptions in the dataset; multi-label handling is especially apt for compound feelings like "tired but happy" that carry two simultaneous emotions. Its multi-label evaluation metrics (subset accuracy, Hamming loss, one-error, ranking loss, average precision) are reusable to score how faithfully text and DALL·E images convey the intended emotion set.  
  *Reusable:* Sentiment140 Twitter corpus (1.6M tweets; ~8000 sampled, 6500 retained, 11,338 sentences annotated; kaggle.com/datasets/kazanova/sentiment140); Subset Accuracy (SA); Hamming Loss (HL); One-Error (OE); Ranking Loss (RL)  
- **Bhat (2024). *Emotion Classification in Short English Texts using Deep Learning Techniques*.** — arXiv preprint (cs.CL) 2024  
  A single-author arXiv preprint benchmarking deep-learning models for five-way emotion classification of short English tweets, introducing the SmallEnglishEmotions dataset.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves strand (a), text-to-emotion NLP: it is a close analogue of the dissertation task - discrete emotion classification of very short English free-text - and offers a concrete, reusable pipeline (DistilBERT transfer learning, Macro-F1/Accuracy evaluation) plus evidence that short texts are actually easier to classify than long ones. Notably it grounds its five emotion categories in Rachael Jack's emotion model, tying the NLP method back to the supervisor Chaona Chen's facial-expression/emotion-perception research lineage.  
  *Reusable:* SmallEnglishEmotions dataset (6372 short English tweets, 5 emotion classes: happiness, sadness, anger, fear, other); Rachael Jack's emotion model (5-category emotion taxonomy used for the label set); Macro-F1 score; Accuracy; Pretrained DistilBERT (distilbert-base) for transfer-learning classification  
- **Niu & Mower Provost (2025). *Contrastive Distillation of Emotion Knowledge from LLMs for Zero-Shot Emotion Recognition*.** — arXiv 2025 (preprint, cs.CL)  
  A contrastive framework that distills GPT-4's free-text emotion descriptions into a compact BERT-sized model for zero-shot emotion recognition across diverse label spaces.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports strand (a) of the dissertation: it is a text-emotion NLP method that turns short free-text into emotion labels across arbitrary schemas without retraining, which fits the 1,025 short emotion descriptions ("tired but happy") that need flexible tagging. Its GPT-4-descriptor-to-label pipeline, multi-label/single-label/dimensional inference modes, and reusable metrics (macro-F1, valence/activation via PCC and Spearman rho) give the student a concrete, citable technique and evaluation vocabulary for extracting emotions from the corpus.  
  *Reusable:* GoEmotions (58k Reddit comments, 27 fine-grained emotion classes); SemEval-2018 Task 1: Affect in Tweets (11-class multi-label); ISEAR - International Survey on Emotion Antecedents and Reactions (7-class single-label); EmoBank (dimensional valence/activation, 5-point scale); Macro-averaged F1 (classification)  
- **Parkington et al. (2025). *Human vs. LLM-Based Thematic Analysis for Digital Mental Health Research: Proof-of-Concept Comparative Study*.** — arXiv 2025 (preprint; IEEE-formatted manuscript)  
  Proof-of-concept comparison of GPT-4o (out-of-the-box and RAG knowledge-base) versus expert human reflexive thematic analysis on mental-health VR interview transcripts.  
  *Why here:* Directly relevant to the dissertation's text-analysis strand (a): it is a concrete methodological template for using an LLM (GPT-4o) with structured prompting to extract emotion-/stress-laden themes and codes from short free-text and to validate the output against human coders (Cohen's kappa, saturation, cost). It also documents where LLMs under-perform — losing nuance, fine-grained emotional triggers and multi-coded meaning — which is a useful caution when the student uses NLP to pull emotions from the 1,025 free-text descriptions. Less central to the folder's 'emotion-lexicons' theme, since it uses generative thematic coding rather than any emotion lexicon.  
  *Reusable:* RISEN prompt-engineering framework (Role, Instructions, Steps, End-Goal, Narrowing); Braun & Clarke reflexive thematic analysis (six-phase methodology); Cohen's kappa (inter-rater reliability; reported kappa = 0.84); GPT-4o (out-of-the-box and RAG knowledge-base configurations); Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for injecting a coding framework  
- **Harutyunyan & Kumar (2026). *Fine-Grained Emotion Detection on GoEmotions: Experimental Comparison of Classical Machine Learning, BiLSTM, and Transformer Models*.** — arXiv 2026 (preprint, submitted to Elsevier)  
  Benchmarks logistic regression, BiLSTM-attention, and fine-tuned BERT for fine-grained 28-label emotion classification on GoEmotions under one unified experimental setup.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports the dissertation's text-to-emotion NLP strand: GoEmotions supplies the fine-grained 27+1 emotion taxonomy well suited to labelling short free-text feeling descriptions, and this paper is a reusable model-selection benchmark showing a strong TF-IDF+logistic-regression baseline versus a fine-tuned BERT ceiling. Its multi-label evaluation recipe (Micro/Macro-F1, Hamming Loss, Subset Accuracy) is a ready template for scoring how well the student's NLP pipeline extracts emotions from the 1,025 short descriptions.  
  *Reusable:* GoEmotions dataset (58,009 Reddit comments, 27 emotions + neutral = 28 labels, Ekman-derived taxonomy); Micro-F1; Macro-F1; Hamming Loss; Subset Accuracy (exact-match)  

## 3. Datasets, benchmarks & lexicons  (13)

- **Mohammad & Turney (2013). *Crowdsourcing a Word–Emotion Association Lexicon*.** — Computational Intelligence, 29(3):436–465, 2013 (Wiley); preprint arXiv:1308.6297  
  Builds the NRC Emotion Lexicon (EmoLex), a large crowdsourced lexicon associating ~10,000 English words with eight basic emotions and positive/negative sentiment.  
  *Why here:* This is the foundational resource for the dissertation's text-to-emotion strand: EmoLex lets the student map each short free-text description (e.g. "tired but happy") onto Plutchik's eight basic emotions plus positive/negative sentiment, giving a lexicon-based, interpretable ground-truth emotion signal to compare against DALL·E image renderings. Its word-sense-level design and documented agreement/annotation methodology also inform how to treat short, low-context inputs and how to justify a lexicon-based NLP pipeline in a psychology-supervised project.  
  *Reusable:* NRC Emotion Lexicon / EmoLex (the released word–emotion association lexicon; word-sense-level emotion + sentiment labels); Plutchik's eight basic emotions (joy, sadness, anger, fear, trust, disgust, surprise, anticipation); Positive/negative (and neutral) polarity / semantic-orientation labels; WordNet Affect Lexicon (WAL); General Inquirer (GI)  
- **Marchewka et al. (2014). *The Nencki Affective Picture System (NAPS): Introduction to a novel, standardized, wide-range, high-quality, realistic picture database*.** — Behavior Research Methods 2014  
  Introduces NAPS, a standardized database of 1,356 realistic photographs with normative valence, arousal, and approach-avoidance ratings for affective research.  
  *Why here:* NAPS supplies a validated, standardized affective-image benchmark and normative valence/arousal/approach-avoidance ratings, plus the SAM and slider-scale rating methodology, all of which the dissertation can reuse for strand (b): quantifying how faithfully the DALL·E-rendered line-art images convey their intended emotions against an established human-rated affective-space framework.  
  *Reusable:* NAPS (Nencki Affective Picture System) image database with normative ratings; IAPS (International Affective Picture System); SAM (Self-Assessment Manikin) rating scale; GAPED (Geneva Affective Picture Database); EmoPicS  
- **Buechel & Hahn (2017). *EmoBank: Studying the Impact of Annotation Perspective and Representation Format on Dimensional Emotion Analysis*.** — Proceedings of EACL 2017 (15th Conf. of the European Chapter of the ACL), Vol. 2 Short Papers, Valencia, Spain  
  Introduces EmoBank, a 10k-sentence English corpus annotated with dimensional Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) emotion metadata from both writer and reader perspectives.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves strand (a): EmoBank is a foundational, reusable VAD-annotated corpus and its writer-vs-reader distinction gives the student a principled way to represent the emotion content of the 1,025 short free-text descriptions dimensionally rather than as discrete labels. The dimensional VAD model also offers a bridge to the psychology framing of supervisor Chaona Chen's facial-expression/emotion-perception work and a quantitative basis for scoring how faithfully DALL·E images convey the intended feeling.  
  *Reusable:* EmoBank corpus (10k / 10,062 sentences, VAD-annotated, writer+reader perspectives; github.com/JULIELab/EmoBank); Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) dimensional emotion model; Self-Assessment Manikin (SAM) rating scale (modified 5-point); Ekman's six Basic Emotions (categorical scheme); MASC — Manually Annotated Sub-Corpus of the American National Corpus  
- **Saravia et al. (2018). *CARER: Contextualized Affect Representations for Emotion Recognition*.** — Proceedings of EMNLP 2018 (Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing), pages 3687-3697, ACL  
  A semi-supervised graph-based method that builds contextualized, pattern-based affect features from tweets to classify text into discrete emotions.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves strand (a) of the dissertation: it supplies both a labelled short-text emotion dataset and a discrete-emotion classification method the student could reuse to extract emotions from the 1,025 free-text descriptions. Its Ekman/Plutchik category scheme and F1 evaluation protocol also give a principled, citable framework for scoring how well emotions are recovered from text before comparing against the DALL-E images.  
  *Reusable:* CARER 'emotion' dataset (English tweets, distant-supervised via 339 hashtags; 8 emotions: anger, anticipation, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, surprise, trust; the 6-emotion subset is the widely reused HuggingFace 'emotion' dataset); F1-score (macro, used due to class imbalance); LIWC affect lexicon; WordNet-Affect synsets; Ekman's six basic emotions  
- **Mohammad et al. (2018). *SemEval-2018 Task 1: Affect in Tweets*.** — Proceedings of the 12th International Workshop on Semantic Evaluation (SemEval-2018), ACL, 2018  
  SemEval-2018 shared task defining benchmark datasets, lexicons, and Pearson-r metrics for detecting emotion categories, intensities, and valence in short multilingual tweets.  
  *Why here:* Supplies the canonical NLP toolkit for strand (a) of the dissertation: a validated categorical/intensity emotion taxonomy, reusable affect lexicons (NRC EmoLex, VAD, Affect Intensity), and the Pearson-r intensity-scoring paradigm for extracting emotions from very short free-text — directly applicable to scoring the 1,025 brief emotion-description phrases (e.g. "tired but happy"). Its documented gender/race bias findings also flag fairness concerns worth noting when interpreting automated emotion extraction.  
  *Reusable:* Affect in Tweets (AIT) Dataset — English/Arabic/Spanish tweets for EI-reg, EI-oc, V-reg, V-oc, E-c; EmoInt / Emotion Intensity Dataset (Tweets-2016); Equity Evaluation Corpus (EEC) — 8,640 English sentences for gender/race bias testing; Best-Worst Scaling (BWS) — comparative annotation method for reliable intensity scores; Pearson correlation coefficient (primary evaluation metric); micro-F1, macro-F1, accuracy for emotion classification (E-c)  
- **Mohammad (2018). *Obtaining Reliable Human Ratings of Valence, Arousal, and Dominance for 20,000 English Words*.** — ACL 2018 (Proceedings of the 56th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, Long Papers)  
  Introduces the NRC VAD Lexicon: reliable human valence/arousal/dominance ratings for ~20,000 English words, obtained via Best-Worst Scaling.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves strand (a) — extracting emotions from the 1,025 short free-text descriptions: the NRC VAD Lexicon lets the student score each phrase (e.g. "tired but happy") on the dimensional valence-arousal-dominance model rather than only discrete categories, and its VAD axes map cleanly onto the psychology framing of supervisor Chaona Chen's facial-expression/emotion-perception work. It is a ready-to-use, freely-available lexicon and a canonical citation for the emotion-lexicon-methods chapter.  
  *Reusable:* NRC VAD Lexicon (valence/arousal/dominance scores 0-1 for 20,007 English words); Best-Worst Scaling (BWS) annotation method; Split-half reliability (SHR) as an annotation-reliability metric; ANEW - Affective Norms of English Words (Bradley & Lang 1999); Warriner et al. (2013) VAD lexicon  
- **Kosti et al. (2020). *Context Based Emotion Recognition using EMOTIC Dataset*.** — IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI) 2020; arXiv 2003.13401  
  Introduces the EMOTIC dataset and a two-branch (person body + scene context) CNN that jointly predicts 26 emotion categories and VAD dimensions from natural images.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves strand (b) of the dissertation: it supplies a validated 26-category emotion taxonomy with plain-language definitions and a VAD continuous framing, plus concrete visual-emotion-recognition evaluation metrics (mean AP, AAE, Jaccard) that the student can reuse to judge how faithfully the DALL-E line-art images convey the intended emotions. Caveat: EMOTIC is built on real photographs where scene context around a bounded person is central, whereas the dissertation's stimuli are context-light black-and-white line drawings, so the model architecture itself transfers less cleanly than the category scheme and evaluation methodology.  
  *Reusable:* EMOTIC dataset (23,571 images / 34,320 people); 26 emotion categories with definitions (Table 1: e.g. Peace, Affection, Engagement, Anticipation, Yearning, Disquietment, Fatigue, Sensitivity, Suffering); VAD continuous model (Valence, Arousal, Dominance), 1-10 scale; Average Precision / mean AP (per-category, higher is better); Average Absolute Error (AAE) per continuous dimension (lower is better)  
- **Achlioptas et al. (2021). *ArtEmis: Affective Language for Visual Art*.** — CVPR 2021 (arXiv:2101.07396)  
  A large-scale dataset (439K utterances over 81K WikiArt paintings) pairing the dominant emotion an artwork evokes with a free-text explanation, plus neural "affective speaker" models that generate emotion-explaining captions.  
  *Why here:* Directly serves both dissertation strands: its 8-category emotion taxonomy and text emotion-classifiers (LSTM/BERT) offer a ready scheme for extracting emotions from the 1,025 short free-text descriptions, while its human-agreement and Turing-test protocols model how to evaluate whether generated images faithfully convey a stated emotion. VADER, TextBlob and the Brysbaert concreteness lexicon are reusable NLP tools, and ArtEmis is a canonical precedent for grounding affective language in visual art.  
  *Reusable:* ArtEmis dataset (439K emotion+explanation annotations, artemisdataset.org); WikiArt image corpus (81,446 artworks, 1,119 artists, 27 styles, 45 genres); 8-emotion taxonomy + 'something-else' (amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness); COCO-Captions (comparison baseline); VADER rule-based sentiment analyzer  
- **Mohamed et al. (2022). *ArtELingo: A Million Emotion Annotations of WikiArt with Emphasis on Diversity over Language and Culture*.** — EMNLP 2022 (arXiv:2211.10780)  
  A large multilingual (Arabic/Chinese/English/Spanish) dataset pairing 80k WikiArt paintings with ~1.2M emotion labels and affective free-text captions.  
  *Why here:* Directly supports the dissertation's 'affective image datasets for art' strand: it is a large gold-standard corpus linking images to both discrete emotion labels and short free-text emotion descriptions, mirroring the dissertation's own pipeline of extracting emotion from text and judging how faithfully an image conveys it. Its 9-class emotion taxonomy, baseline emotion-classification/caption-generation models, and evidence that emotion-from-text varies by phrasing/culture provide a reusable framework and a cautionary note for evaluating whether DALL-E line-art images faithfully render short emotion phrases.  
  *Reusable:* ArtELingo dataset (public, artelingo.org; standard splits + baseline models); ArtEmis dataset (predecessor: 0.45M English emotion labels + affective captions on 80k WikiArt artworks); WikiArt image collection (~80k artworks, artist-by-nation metadata); 9-emotion taxonomy: Contentment, Awe, Amusement, Excitement (positive); Sadness, Fear, Disgust, Anger (negative); Other; COCO (used as a factual-caption comparison baseline)  
- **Yang et al. (2023). *EmoSet: A Large-scale Visual Emotion Dataset with Rich Attributes*.** — ICCV 2023 (IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision)  
  A 3.3M-image (118K human-annotated) visual emotion dataset labelled with 8 Mikels emotion categories plus 6 describable emotion attributes.  
  *Why here:* Directly relevant to strand (b) of the dissertation — evaluating how faithfully images convey emotion — because EmoSet supplies a validated, balanced taxonomy (8 Mikels categories, positive/negative polarity) and a set of psychologically-motivated visual attributes that could frame or benchmark whether the DALL·E line-art images register the intended feelings. Its finding that brightness, colorfulness, scene, and facial expression correlate strongly with perceived emotion offers concrete, reusable image features for assessing the black-and-white line-art renders.  
  *Reusable:* EmoSet (EmoSet-3.3M and EmoSet-118K); Mikels 8-category emotion model (amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness); Ekman 6-emotion / facial-expression model; Six emotion attributes: brightness, colorfulness, scene type, object class, facial expression, human action; FI dataset  
- **Ghosh et al. (2023). *GenEval: An Object-Focused Framework for Evaluating Text-to-Image Alignment*.** — NeurIPS 2023 (Track on Datasets and Benchmarks)
