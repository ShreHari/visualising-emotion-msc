# Research Gap: Text-to-Image Emotional Fidelity for Sparse, Mixed-Affect Self-Reports

## 1. What the evidence establishes

**(i) Emotion has no single canonical structure, and self-report specifically fractures into fine-grained, often blended categories rather than the six-way scheme most tools assume.** The two load-bearing traditions — Ekman's discrete basic emotions (Ekman 1992) and Russell's valence–arousal circumplex (Russell 1980; Posner, Russell & Peterson 2005) — are both only partial. Cowen & Keltner (2017) show that free self-report reliably resolves *27* categories bridged by continuous gradients, with categorical labels explaining more variance (~78%) than affective dimensions (~61%), arguing against both strict basic-emotion and purely dimensional accounts. Crucially for this project, the supervisor's own line of work (Jack et al. 2012; Chen et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2024) demonstrates that even *facial* emotion signalling is culturally relative and decomposes into distinct "classifier" (which emotion) and "intensifier" (how intense) components — i.e. emotion representation is neither universal nor one-dimensional.

**(ii) Emotion-from-text NLP is mature on social-media/third-party corpora but demonstrably weakest on exactly the input this dataset contains: short, first-person, low-frequency, mixed-affect utterances.** GoEmotions establishes the 27-category benchmark with a BERT baseline of only macro-F1 .46, collapsing to ~.00–.21 on low-frequency, verbally implicit emotions (Demszky et al. 2020; corroborated by Harutyunyan & Kumar 2026). Performance on *self-reported* ISEAR-style data is systematically poor, attributed to a first-party vs third-party annotation mismatch and rare shame/guilt labels (Yang et al. 2023, "Context Unlocks Emotions"). Dimensional lexicons (NRC-VAD; Mohammad 2018, 2025) and writer-vs-reader-perspective corpora (EmoBank; Buechel & Hahn 2017) offer a continuous alternative better suited to blended affect, and reviews confirm no accepted model and a scarcity of balanced labelled data (Seyeditabari et al. 2018).

**(iii) Text-to-image models render prompts fluently but have known, measured weaknesses in fine detail, compositional binding, and faces.** The DALL·E / diffusion lineage (Ramesh et al. 2021, 2022; GLIDE, Nichol et al. 2021; Imagen, Saharia et al. 2022; SDXL, Podell et al. 2023; DALL·E 3, Betker et al. 2023) improves prompt-following via better/synthetic captions, but Betker et al. (2023) explicitly flag weak spatial placement, unreliable text rendering, and hallucination, and DALL·E 3 internally *rewrites* prompts before generation.

**(iv) Automatic T2I evaluation measures object/compositional alignment — not affect — and even there it is unstable.** The dominant metrics evaluate whether objects, attributes, counts, and spatial relations from the prompt appear in the image: CLIPScore (Hessel et al. 2021), TIFA (Hu et al. 2023), Davidsonian Scene Graph (Cho et al. 2024), GenEval (Ghosh et al. 2023), and VQAScore (Lin et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024). The survey by Hartwig et al. (2024) makes the scope explicit — compositional quality is decomposed into object accuracy, spatial, non-spatial, and attribute binding — and concludes there is *no single general-purpose T2I metric*. None of these evaluates emotional content. Reliability is itself contested: human–automatic correlations are weak, especially for photorealism/aesthetics (HEIM; Lee et al. 2023), and Zhang & Wan (2025) show CLIPScore and DSGScore fail worst-case robustness under a 1-pixel perturbation and produce seed-inconsistent model rankings, with VQAScore comparatively (but not perfectly) more stable.

**(v) Emotion *can* be generated into and recognised from images, but every existing system is target-conditioned, colour-dependent, and trained on photographs or art — never sparse monochrome line-art.** Affective generation (EmoGen, Yang et al. 2024; EmoEdit, Yang et al. 2024; Make Me Happier/EmoEditor, Lin et al. 2024; EmotiCrafter, Dang et al. 2025) conditions on a *target* emotion label or valence–arousal coordinate and renders full-colour scenes; Make Me Happier reports that it "struggles with fine facial detail" and that positive emotions are harder to distinguish than negative. Decisively, Alvarado (2025) shows that in T2I output *lightness and chroma* (not hue) are the primary carriers of emotional meaning, with red the main exception — i.e. colour is the dominant affective channel. Visual-emotion recognition is likewise built on natural photographs and paintings (IAPS, EmoSet — Yang et al. 2023; ArtPhoto/Abstract — Machajdik & Hanbury 2010; WikiArt/ArtELingo — Mohamed et al. 2022), and is beset by an affective gap, subjectivity, dataset bias and domain shift (Zhao et al. 2021; Panda et al. 2018).

**(vi) Machine recognition of image emotion is fragile and confounds "expressed" with "evoked" affect, while humans detect and read AI images imperfectly.** VLMs are "largely inept" at zero-shot evoked-emotion recognition, show a systematic sentiment bias toward positive labels, and are highly sensitive to label ordering (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025); MLLMs handle valence but fail on arousal/dominance and conflate the subject's expressed emotion with the viewer's evoked emotion (EEmo-Bench; Gao et al. 2025). On the human side, people detect AI images only ~62% of the time (Roca et al. 2025) and read short-prompt images differently from long-prompt ones (Moeßner & Adel 2024). The one study that rates *emotional* alignment with human judges — Lomas et al. (2024) — finds AI model is the dominant factor (DALL·E 3 ≫ DALL·E 2 ≫ SD v1), that emotions on people align better than on robots, and that alignment varies sharply by emotion (shock/surprise best; resentment worst).

## 2. The convergence point

These strands intersect at a single unstudied object. Emotion theory says short self-reports carry fine-grained, frequently *blended* affect best captured categorically-plus-dimensionally (Cowen & Keltner 2017; Russell 1980). Text-NLP says that this exact register — short, first-person, low-frequency, mixed — is where classifiers are weakest (Demszky et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2023). T2I generation says the models will render such prompts but wobble on faces, detail and (via prompt-rewriting) on fidelity (Betker et al. 2023; Lin et al. 2024). Automatic evaluation says our best metrics score *objects*, not *affect*, and are themselves unstable (Hartwig et al. 2024; Zhang & Wan 2025). Emotion-in-image work says affect is generatable and recognisable **but primarily through colour** (Alvarado 2025) and **only on photographs/art**, with recognisers biased and evoked≠expressed (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025; Gao et al. 2025). The collective implication: whether a text-to-image model faithfully conveys the affect of an unmediated, mixed-affect self-report **once colour is removed** is not answered by any existing metric, dataset, or study — it sits precisely in the hole between them.

## 3. The gap, stated precisely

**No existing work measures the text→image *emotional* fidelity of short, unmediated, mixed-affect first-person self-reports rendered as colour-free line-art, nor characterises how stable that conveyed emotion is under identical prompts.** The gap is sharp against each adjacent literature:

- **vs. automatic T2I alignment (VQAScore, TIFA, GenEval, DSG; Lin et al. 2024; Hu et al. 2023; Ghosh et al. 2023; Cho et al. 2024).** These verify presence of *objects/attributes/relations*, not affect. A line-art image can score perfectly on "is there a person?" while conveying the wrong emotion entirely. Compositional metrics are, by their own taxonomy (Hartwig et al. 2024), silent on valence and arousal.
- **vs. Lomas et al. (2024), the closest prior art.** Lomas et al. rate emotional alignment with human judges — but on *templated* prompts: a fixed 10-item "Emotion Typology," each single emotion rendered onto a "person" or "robot." This dataset's prompts are the opposite: unmediated public self-reports, median ~4 words, frequently *mixed* ("tired but happy"), not drawn from a controlled typology and not scaffolded onto a face.
- **vs. affective image generation (EmoGen, EmoEdit, Make Me Happier, EmotiCrafter).** These are *conditional generators* steered by a target emotion or V–A coordinate and evaluated in full colour. The present study is *observational* — the emotion is latent in real self-report text, not a control knob — and colour, shown to be the dominant affective carrier (Alvarado 2025), is deliberately absent.
- **vs. visual-emotion recognition (EmoSet, IAPS, WikiArt/ArtELingo; Yang et al. 2023; Machajdik & Hanbury 2010; Mohamed et al. 2022).** These classify photographs and paintings, richly coloured and textured. Sparse monochrome line-art is out-of-distribution for every one of these recognisers, and none addresses the expressed-vs-evoked distinction for such stimuli.

**Honest novelty accounting.** What is *genuinely novel* is (a) the object of study — an unmediated public self-report corpus rendered as colour-stripped line-art, isolating whether affect survives without colour; (b) end-to-end text→image *affect* fidelity measured on non-templated, mixed-valence input; and (c) the 66 duplicate-prompt runs, which turn generative variability of *conveyed emotion* into a natural experiment — something the conditional-generation and Lomas-style rating studies do not do. What is *incremental* is the toolkit: the fidelity pipeline recombines an existing text-emotion model, an existing image-emotion recogniser, and existing agreement statistics; human alignment rating follows Lomas et al. (2024). The contribution is therefore the *corpus + question + consistency experiment*, not a new metric — and it should be framed that way to an examiner.

## 4. Why this dataset is uniquely suited to close it

- **Unmediated public self-report** removes the templating that limits Lomas et al. (2024) and the target-conditioning that limits EmoGen/EmotiCrafter — the affect is real, in-the-wild, and writer-perspective (the register EmoBank and Yang et al. 2023 flag as distinct and hard).
- **Short, mixed-affect text (median 4 words, "tired but happy")** lands exactly on the weak spot of categorical NLP (Demszky et al. 2020) and motivates a *dimensional* valence–arousal treatment (Russell 1980; NRC-VAD, Mohammad 2018) that can represent blends a single label cannot — making mixed affect a feature to study, not noise to discard.
- **Monochrome line-art strips colour as a confound.** Because Alvarado (2025) shows lightness/chroma/red carry most T2I emotional meaning, removing colour is a clean manipulation: it isolates whether *form, posture, and line* alone can carry affect, and predicts which emotions should survive (those not colour-dependent) versus collapse.
- **66 duplicate-prompt runs are a built-in natural experiment** on generative emotional consistency — identical prompt, repeated generation — enabling a variance analysis of *conveyed affect* that no conditional-generation study performs.
- **Recoverable DALL·E timestamps** allow the consistency and fidelity analyses to be checked against generation date/model drift, a lightweight longitudinal control.

## 5. Concrete, answerable research questions

**RQ1 — Text→image emotional fidelity.** *Does the emotion recoverable from the line-art image match the emotion in the source self-report?* Extract the text label two ways — categorically via a BERT model fine-tuned on the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al. 2020; Harutyunyan & Kumar 2026) and dimensionally via NRC-VAD lexicon scores mapped onto the valence–arousal circumplex (Mohammad 2018; Russell 1980). Extract the image label via an EmoSet-trained classifier (Yang et al. 2023) and/or a VLM probe (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025; Gao et al. 2025). Quantify agreement with macro-F1 and Cohen's κ (categorical) and V/A error and Spearman ρ (dimensional), and report cross-modal prompt-vs-image emotion correlation as in Mehta & Buntain (2024). Anchor the automatic result to human judgement using the 0–10 alignment rating protocol of Lomas et al. (2024).

**RQ2 — Which emotions survive colour removal?** *Given that colour is the dominant affective channel in T2I (Alvarado 2025), which emotions remain recoverable in monochrome line-art and which collapse?* Break RQ1 fidelity down per emotion and by valence/arousal quadrant; test the specific predictions that colour-dependent affects degrade most, that positive emotions are harder to distinguish than negative (Make Me Happier; Lin et al. 2024), and that recognisers exhibit a positive-sentiment bias (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025) — using confusion matrices plus human ratings as the arbiter.

**RQ3 — Generative emotional consistency.** *Across the 66 duplicate-prompt runs, how stable is the conveyed emotion under an identical prompt?* For each duplicated prompt compute pairwise image-emotion agreement — classifier-distribution divergence (KL, as used in the S_emo term of Make Me Happier; Lin et al. 2024) and/or VQAScore/emotion-probe variance — alongside variance in human alignment ratings, and relate any drift to recoverable timestamps. This directly measures generative variance of *affect*, distinct from Zhang & Wan's (2025) metric-level seed robustness.

## 6. Threats and honest caveats

- **Metric instability (Zhang & Wan 2025).** CLIPScore and DSGScore fail 1-pixel robustness and give seed-inconsistent rankings; a statistically significant metric gap can correspond to a ~50% dominance ratio. Do not rest conclusions on a single automatic metric; report robustness and effect sizes, and prefer the comparatively more stable VQAScore while acknowledging it is not immune.
- **Metrics are weak on fine detail and faces — exactly what line-art foregrounds.** TIFA scores drop once prompts exceed ~5 entities (Hu et al. 2023); VQAScore/compositional metrics target objects, not affect (Li et al. 2024; Hartwig et al. 2024); Make Me Happier itself "struggles with fine facial detail" (Lin et al. 2024). A line-art face is a fine-detail, low-signal stimulus for every one of these tools.
- **Label bias and evoked-vs-expressed confound in image recognition.** VLMs prefer positive labels and are sensitive to label ordering (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025); MLLMs fail on arousal/dominance and conflate the subject's expressed emotion with the viewer's evoked emotion (Gao et al. 2025). The study must pre-commit to which construct it measures and control label-set order.
- **Human ground truth is itself noisy and culturally variable.** Even the "gold" human ratings carry label noise and subjectivity (Bhattacharyya & Wang 2025 EC-II/III; Machajdik & Hanbury 2010), titles shift affective response (WikiArt Emotions; Mohammad & Kiritchenko 2018), and emotion perception is culturally relative (Jack et al. 2012; Chen et al. 2024; ArtELingo, Mohamed et al. 2022). Rater demographics and agreement (à la Lomas et al.'s inter-rater correlation) must be reported.
- **The text "ground-truth" emotion is uncertain.** Short, low-frequency, mixed self-reports are the worst case for categorical NLP (Demszky et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2023), so fidelity error will partly reflect *text-side* labelling error. A dimensional VAD treatment (Mohammad 2018; Buechel & Hahn 2017) mitigates but does not eliminate this — propagate the uncertainty rather than treating the text label as fixed truth.
- **Recogniser domain shift onto line-art.** EmoSet/IAPS-trained classifiers and VLMs are built on colour photographs and art and suffer severe cross-dataset drops (Zhao et al. 2021; Panda et al. 2018); their scores on monochrome line-art are out-of-distribution and must be validated against human labels before being trusted. Note too that EmoSet has no neutral class (Mehta & Buntain 2024), a poor fit for neutral/mixed self-reports.
- **No seed and no recoverable rewritten prompt for exact regeneration.** DALL·E 3 internally upsamples/rewrites prompts (Betker et al. 2023) and no generation seed is stored, so the 66 duplicates measure *end-to-end* variance but cannot decompose it into prompt-rewriting vs sampling stochasticity, and exact regeneration is impossible. Timestamps are recoverable; seeds and intermediate prompts are not — state this as a hard limit on the consistency analysis.
